**Darjeeling** is a town and municipality in the Indian state of West Bengal. It is located in the Lesser Himalayas at an average elevation of 2,042 meters (6,700 ft). 

Renowned for its majestic views of the **Kangchenjunga** (the world's third-highest mountain peak), its premium black tea plantations, and the historic **Darjeeling Himalayan Railway**, the town was developed as a health resort and "sanatorium" by the British East India Company in the mid-19th century. Today, it remains one of India's most popular tourism destinations, offering a unique blend of colonial charm, Nepalese culture, and Buddhist heritage.

## History

Before the early 19th century, the mountainous region of Darjeeling was ruled intermittently by the Kingdom of Sikkim and the Kingdom of Nepal. 

In 1828, British East India Company officials Arthur Campbell and Lloyd visited the area while searching for a direct route to Tibet. They realized the location's potential as a sanatorium for British soldiers suffering from tropical heat in the plains. In 1835, the Chogyal of Sikkim signed a deed of grant ceding Darjeeling to the British in exchange for an annual allowance.

The British introduced tea cultivation to the region in 1841, led by Dr. Campbell, who planted Chinese tea seeds (*Camellia sinensis*) in his private garden. The experiment was highly successful, leading to the rapid development of large tea estates.

## The Darjeeling Himalayan Railway (Toy Train)

Completed in 1881, the **Darjeeling Himalayan Railway (DHR)** is a narrow-gauge railway that climbs from Siliguri in the plains to Ghoom (the highest station in India at 7,407 ft) and descends into Darjeeling. 

The DHR is a masterpiece of mountain engineering, utilizing clever loops (such as the famous **Batasia Loop**) and zig-zag reverses to climb steep gradients without tunnels. In 1999, UNESCO declared the DHR a **World Heritage Site**, recognizing it as an outstanding example of a scenic, functional transport link that retains its original character.

## Darjeeling Tea

Often hailed as the **"Champagne of Teas"**, Darjeeling tea is globally celebrated for its unique muscatel flavor and delicate floral aroma. 

The tea's distinctive taste is a product of Darjeeling's unique terroir: high altitudes, steep slopes, rich acidic soil, heavy rainfall, and misty mornings. In 2004, Darjeeling Tea became the first Indian product to receive a **Geographical Indication (GI)** tag, ensuring that only tea grown in designated gardens in the Darjeeling district can carry the name.

## Culture and Demographics

Darjeeling has a highly diverse population, with a majority of ethnic Nepalis, alongside native Lepchas, Bhutias, and Tibetan refugees. 

The prominent religions are Hinduism and Tibetan Buddhism. The town is dotted with historic monasteries, such as the **Ghoom Monastery**, which houses a 15-foot statue of the Maitreya Buddha. Nepali is the primary language spoken, alongside Bengali, Hindi, and English.
