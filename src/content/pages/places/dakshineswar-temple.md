The **Dakshineswar Kali Temple** is a historic Hindu temple complex located in Dakshineswar, on the eastern bank of the Hooghly River (a distributary of the Ganges) just north of Kolkata, West Bengal. 

The temple is dedicated to **Bhavatarini**, an aspect of the Goddess Kali, representing "She who takes Her devotees across the ocean of existence." 

Constructed in 1855 by the legendary philanthropist and social reformer **Rani Rashmoni**, the temple is famous worldwide as the place where the legendary 19th-century mystic saint **Ramakrishna Paramahansa** achieved his spiritual realizations and lived for several decades.

## History of Foundation

In the mid-19th century, Rani Rashmoni, a wealthy widow from a low-caste Zamindar family, resolved to go on a pilgrimage to the holy city of Varanasi to offer worship. According to temple records, on the night before her departure, she had a vivid dream of the Goddess Kali, who told her:

> *"There is no need to go to Varanasi. Install my image in a beautiful temple on the banks of the Ganges River and arrange for my worship there. I shall manifest myself in the image and accept your devotion."*

Rani Rashmoni immediately cancelled her pilgrimage and purchased a 30-acre plot of land in Dakshineswar, which was then a quiet village. The temple complex took eight years to build, costing an enormous sum of nine hundred thousand rupees, and was officially consecrated on 31 May 1855.

Because Rani Rashmoni was from a low caste, orthodox Brahmin priests initially refused to serve as priests at her temple. She overcame this obstacle by hiring Ramkumar Chattopadhyay, a liberal Brahmin scholar, as the head priest. Following Ramkumar's death in 1856, his younger brother, **Gadadhar** (who later became world-famous as **Ramakrishna Paramahansa**), took charge of the shrine.

## Architectural Design

The temple complex is built in the traditional **Navaratna** (nine-spired) architectural style of Bengal:
* **The Main Temple**: A grand three-story structure standing on a high platform, topped by nine spires. The inner sanctum houses a beautiful basalt stone idol of Bhavatarini standing on a white marble statue of Lord Shiva.
* **The Twelve Shiva Temples**: A row of twelve identical traditional *Aat-chala* (eight-roofed) shrines dedicated to Lord Shiva face the river on the temple's expansive brick courtyard.
* **Radha-Krishna Temple**: A separate shrine located in the complex dedicated to Radha and Krishna.
* **The Ghat & Kuthi**: A grand bathing ghat leading into the Hooghly River, and the *Kuthi* (bungalow) where Rani Rashmoni and her family stayed.

## Ramakrishna's Room and Panchavati

The temple complex includes several spots deeply associated with Ramakrishna's daily life and spiritual practices:
1. **Ramakrishna's Room**: The simple room in the northwestern corner of the courtyard where the saint lived, slept, and hosted spiritual seekers (including Swami Vivekananda and Keshab Chandra Sen).
2. **Panchavati**: A grove of five sacred trees (Banyan, Peepal, Neem, Amalaki, and Bilva) planted under Ramakrishna's directions, where he spent hours in intense meditation (*Sadhana*) and achieved his highest spiritual states.
