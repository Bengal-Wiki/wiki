**Bishnupur** (also spelled **Vishnupur**) is a town and municipality in the Bankura district of West Bengal, India. It is globally renowned for its magnificent **terracotta temples** built in the 17th and 18th centuries by the kings of the **Mallabhum dynasty**, as well as its heritage of Hindustani classical music and exquisite handloom Baluchari silk sarees.

Because stone was extremely scarce in the alluvial plains of Bengal, local architects and craftsmen turned to baking bricks and moulding panels of local alluvial clay to construct and decorate temples. The resulting terracotta structures represent the peak of medieval Bengali vernacular architecture.

## History of the Malla Dynasty

For nearly a thousand years, the Bankura-Bishnupur region was ruled by the Malla kings of Mallabhum. The dynasty was founded in 694 CE by Adi Malla. 

Bishnupur was made the capital of the kingdom in 994 CE under King Jagat Malla. The kingdom reached its golden era in the late 16th and 17th centuries under **King Bir Hambir** and his successors. Following Bir Hambir's conversion to Vaishnavism by the saint Srinivasa Acharya, Bishnupur became a major center of Krishna devotion, leading to the construction of numerous temples dedicated to Radha and Krishna.

## Significant Terracotta Temples

Bishnupur's temples are characterized by their unique curved roofs (mimicking traditional Bengali thatched huts, known as the *chala* style) and elaborate wall panels depicting scenes from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and daily life.

### 1. Rasmancha

Built by King Bir Hambir in 1600 CE, the **Rasmancha** is the oldest standing brick monument in Bishnupur. It is structurally unique, featuring a pyramidal roof surrounded by small hut-like arches. During the annual Vaishnava Ras festival, idols of Radha and Krishna from all temples in the town were brought here to be exhibited for public worship.

### 2. Shyamrai Temple (Five-Spired Temple)

Built by King Raghunath Singha in 1643 CE, this temple features a unique *Pancha-ratna* (five pinnacles) design. The entire surface—both interior and exterior walls—is covered in extremely fine, detailed terracotta tiles depicting the *Raslila* (divine dance of Krishna), musical ensembles, and battle scenes.

### 3. Jorebangla Temple

Also constructed by King Raghunath Singha in 1655 CE, the **Jorebangla Temple** is a masterpiece of the double-hut style. It consists of two joined traditional *do-chala* (two-roofed) structures: one serving as a porch and the other as the inner shrine (*garbhagriha*), topped by a small spire.

## Baluchari Silk Sarees

Bishnupur is the primary weaving center of the famous **Baluchari Sarees**, made from fine silk. The sarees are distinguished by their elaborate borders and *pallu* (drape end), which feature intricately woven jacquard designs depicting scenes from Indian mythology, royal court gatherings, and colonial figures. In 2011, the Baluchari Saree was granted a Geographical Indication (GI) tag.

## The Bishnupur Gharana

Bishnupur is also home to the **Bishnupur Gharana**, the only traditional school of Hindustani classical music in Bengal. Developed under the royal patronage of the Malla kings, it is renowned for its emphasis on the classical, austere **Dhrupad** style of singing, free from ornate embellishments, and its mastery of the veena and pakhawaj.
