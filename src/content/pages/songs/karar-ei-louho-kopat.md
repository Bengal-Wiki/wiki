**Karar Ei Louho Kopat** (Bengali: কারার ঐ লৌহ-কবাট, lit. "Those Iron Gates of Prison") is a legendary Bengali revolutionary marching song written and composed by the Rebel Poet **Kazi Nazrul Islam** in 1922. 

The song is one of the most powerful anti-colonial musical compositions in Indian history. Written during Nazrul's imprisonment by the British colonial administration, its lyrics served as an explosive call to arms, urging freedom fighters to smash the iron gates of British prisons, break their chains, and pull down the edifices of colonial oppression.

## History and Composition

In 1922, the British government launched a severe crackdown on nationalist publications. Nazrul was arrested in Asansol for his anti-colonial poem *Anandamayir Agamane* and placed in Alipore Central Jail in Calcutta.

While imprisoned, Nazrul witnessed the brutal tortures inflicted upon political prisoners. Despising the passive containment, he composed *Karar Ei Louho Kopat* to keep the spirits of the prisoners high. The song was smuggled out of jail and immediately published in the journal *Banglar Bani* in 1922, spreading like wildfire across the student and revolutionary committees of undivided Bengal.

The song is set to a highly energetic, fast-paced marching military rhythm, which gave it an intense mobilizational power. It was banned repeatedly by the British Raj, but continued to be sung secretly during mass demonstrations.

The lyrics are written in a powerful, direct, and colloquial Bengali dialect:

ধ্বংস-নিশান  
উড়ুক প্রাচীর প্রাচীর ভেদি॥  

গাজনের বাজনা বাজা!

কে দেয় সাজা

শিখায় এ হীন তথ্য কে রে?  

ওরে ও পাগলা ভোলা!  
দে রে দে দেউলে দোলা!  
রক্তে দোলা

মুক্তির বন্দিনী রে

যত সব বন্দী-শালায়—  
আগুন জ্বালা,  
আগুন জ্বালা, ফেল্ উপাড়ি॥
