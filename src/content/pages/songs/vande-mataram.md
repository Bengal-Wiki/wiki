**Vande Mataram** (Bengali: বন্দে মাতরম্, lit. "I bow to thee, Mother") is a Sanskrit poem written by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** in the 1870s, which he included in his 1882 Bengali novel *Anandamath*. 

The poem was a popular marching song during the Indian independence movement. It served as a powerful rallying cry for nationalists protesting British rule. In recognition of its historical role, the Constituent Assembly of India adopted the first two stanzas of the song as the **National Song of India** on 24 January 1950, declaring that it should carry "equal status" with India's national anthem, *Jana Gana Mana*.

## History and Significance

Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay wrote the poem at a time when British administrators were introducing patriotic anthems like *God Save the Queen* in colonial India. He envisioned *Vande Mataram* as a tribute to the Motherland, personified as the Goddess Durga.

The song gained national prominence during the anti-partition Swadeshi movement in Bengal (1905). It became a powerful political slogan, and singing it in public was banned by the British colonial government, leading to thousands of arrests. 

At the 1896 Calcutta session of the Indian National Congress, **Rabindranath Tagore** sang *Vande Mataram* publicly for the first time, having set it to a beautiful classical melody in Raga Desh.

## Complete Lyrics

The official national song comprises the first two stanzas of the poem:

### Sanskrit/Bengali Script (Original)

বন্দে মাতরম্।  
সুজলাং সুফলাং মলয়জশীতলাম্,  
শস্যশ্যামলাং মাতরম্।  
বন্দে মাতরম্॥  

শুভ্রজ্যোত্স্না-পুলকিত-যামিনীম্,  
ফুল্লকুসুমিত-দ্রুমদলশোভিনীম্,  
সুহাসিনীং সুমধুরভাষিণীম্,  
সুখদাং বরদাং মাতরম্॥  
বন্দে মাতরম্॥  

### Roman Transliteration

Vande Mataram!  
Sujalam, sufalam, malayaja shitalam,  
Shasya shyamalam, Mataram!  
Vande Mataram!  

Shubhra jyotsna pulakita yaminim,  
Phulla kusumita drumadala shobhinim,  
Suhasinim sumadhura bhashinim,  
Sukhadam varadam, Mataram!  
Vande Mataram!  

### English Translation by Sri Aurobindo

> I bow to thee, Mother,  
> richly-watered, richly-fruited,  
> cool with the winds of the south,  
> dark with the crops of the harvests,  
> The Mother!  
> 
> Her nights rejoicing in the glory of the moonlight,  
> her lands clothed beautifully with her trees in flowering bloom,  
> sweet of laughter, sweet of speech,  
> The Mother, giver of boons, giver of bliss!
