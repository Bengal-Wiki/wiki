**Rabindranath Tagore** (7 May 1861 – 7 August 1941) was a Bengali polymath who reshaped Bengali literature and music, as well as Indian art with Contextual Modernism in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Author of the "profoundly sensitive, fresh and beautiful verse" of *Gitanjali*, he became in 1913 the first non-European and the first lyricist to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. Tagore's poetic songs were viewed as spiritual and mercurial; however, his "elegant prose and magical poetry" remain largely unknown outside Bengal. He is sometimes referred to as "the Bard of Bengal" and "Gurudev".

A Pirali Brahmin from Calcutta with ancestral gentry roots in Jessore, Tagore wrote poetry as an eight-year-old. At the age of sixteen, he released his first substantial poems under the pseudonym *Bhanusimha* ("Sun Lion"), which were grabbed by literary authorities as long-lost classics. By 1877 he graduated to his first short stories and dramas, published under his real name. 

## Early Life and Education

Tagore was born at the Jorasanko mansion in Calcutta, the youngest of thirteen surviving children of Debendranath Tagore (1817–1905) and Sarada Devi (1830–1875). Debendranath had formulated the Brahmo faith propagated by his friend Raja Ram Mohan Roy, and took charge of the Brahmo Samaj after Roy's death.

Rabindranath was raised mostly by servants; his mother had died in his early childhood and his father travelled widely. The Tagore family was at the forefront of the Bengal Renaissance. They hosted the publication of literary magazines, held classical and Bengali music recitals, and were deeply involved in social and religious reform movements.

## Literary and Musical Career

Tagore's literary career spanned more than six decades. He wrote in multiple genres, including poetry, short stories, novels, dramas, essays, and travelogues.

### Poetry and Songs (Rabindra Sangeet)

Tagore wrote approximately 2,230 songs, collectively known as *Rabindra Sangeet* (Tagore Songs). They are characterized by their distinctive blending of classical Indian music with traditional Bengali folk tunes (particularly Baul music). His songs address themes of love, nature, spiritual devotion, and patriotism. 

His compositions are the national anthems of two sovereign nations:
1. **India**: *Jana Gana Mana* (Thou Art the Ruler of the Minds of All People)
2. **Bangladesh**: *Amar Shonar Bangla* (My Golden Bengal)

Additionally, the national anthem of Sri Lanka (*Sri Lanka Matha*) was heavily inspired and influenced by his style and words, written by his student Ananda Samarakoon.

### Novels and Short Stories

Tagore introduced the short story genre to Bengali literature. His stories, such as *Kabuliwala*, *The Postmaster*, and *Nastanirh* (The Broken Nest), depict the lives of ordinary people, rural landscapes, and the complexities of human relationships.

His notable novels include:
* *Gora*: A complex exploration of Indian identity, nationalism, and religious orthodoxy.
* *Ghare-Baire* (The Home and the World): Set against the backdrop of the Swadeshi movement, exploring the clash between traditional values and modern nationalism.

## Founder of Shantiniketan

In 1901, Tagore moved to Santiniketan to build an ashram with a marble-floored prayer hall, an experimental school, gardens, groves, and a library. There he established the school *Patha Bhavana*, which emphasized learning in natural surroundings rather than rigid classrooms.

In 1921, Tagore founded **Visva-Bharati University**, which he envisioned as a global center for the study of humanities, bridging Eastern and Western cultures. He used the Nobel Prize money and royalties to fund the institution's growth.

## Legacy and Impact

Tagore remains an iconic figure in Bengali culture. His birthday (25th of Baisakh in the Bengali calendar, usually falling on 8 or 9 May) is celebrated globally as *Rabindra Jayanti*. His works continue to be adapted into films, theatre productions, and musical recitals, most notably by filmmaker Satyajit Ray.
